Quarantined Feedback: Stop Feeding the Model Its Own Shadow

Audience: Mid-to-senior ML/platform engineers • Pattern: Quarantined Feedback • Time to read: 6 min

Three Non-Obvious Insights

  • Human approval is not a breaker — humans anchor to model outputs.
  • Retraining frequency amplifies contaminated variance.
  • Warehouse write is where collapse begins, not training start.

Opening Scene

January 14, 2026, 10:20 UTC: GitHub's Copilot gateway starts routing to a Claude Opus 4.5 build that never passed review. It promoted itself. A nightly job counted thumbs-up from Copilot Chat, saw a spike, and pushed. The spike was Copilot approving Copilot. The warehouse ate itself.

Like ouroboros learning to swallow faster.

Bold Reframe

[BEAT 1] Your model isn't learning from the world. It's learning from its own shadow in your warehouse.

Feedback loops don't improve models. They launder mistakes. The dominant advice — close the loop, collect production feedback, retrain nightly — turns your warehouse into a photocopier when your model influenced the data. The fix isn't better training. It's treating your own output as untrusted third-party data.

Pattern Card — Quarantined Feedback

Problem
Production signals are contaminated by prior model actions; retraining amplifies error.
Forces
Cost wants frequent retrains, latency wants auto-promote, accuracy wants clean labels. Pick two.
Solution
Provenance tag + quarantine table + circuit breaker on promotion + 1% self-consumption cap.
Consequences
Slower but stable retrains, measurable Self-Consumption Ratio, golden set stops drift.

Hidden Links

[BEAT 2] The fix isn't a better model. It's a quarantine line around your own data.

Feedback loops are not a training problem. They are a supply chain attack you commit against yourself.

Framework / Checklist

  • Tag every warehouse row with influenced_by_model at write time.
  • Create feedback_quarantine → feedback_trusted promotion with execution-based validator.
  • Cap self-consumption at 1% per training cycle, freeze golden set.
  • Measure Self-Consumption Ratio daily. Alert >1%.
